In plain terms

The luck score works like a coin-flip test. Getting 7 heads in 10 flips is normal, and nobody would call the coin rigged. Getting 700 heads in 1,000 flips is a different story. Nine of the ten machines are in "7 out of 10" territory. M-1158 is in "700 out of 1,000" territory: it pays a win on 39% of spins when its design says 32%, month after month.

So what: don't replace machines for being unlucky. Have a technician check M-1158's settings.

Appendix

How we did it

How we did it, in four lines

  • Fair comparison: we only count days a machine was on the floor and working.
  • Luck removed: we compare what each machine kept with what its game design says it should keep.
  • Too strange to be luck: a machine is flagged only if its results would happen by chance less than 1 time in 700.
  • Honest forecast: we built it on January–April and tested it on May–June, which it had never seen.

AI use, and how we checked it

Our ideas: the approach (testing luck vs. location vs. leak before spending), the decision to keep most of the budget, and the user experience of explaining the results to many audiences at once: the story intro and the Simple / Analyst views.

Full method (definitions, formulas, limits)

Money definitions

Amount bet (coin-in) = all wagers including re-bet winnings. House win = amount bet − payout. Expected earnings (theo) = Σ bet × (1 − payback rate). Keep rate (hold) = Σ house win ÷ Σ amount bet. Ratios are always computed from totals, never by averaging daily ratios.

Fair comparisons

Days a machine wasn't installed yet, or was already retired, are left out (not counted as zero). "Per working hour" divides by hours the machine was available, which removes downtime. March 8 had 23 hours (daylight saving); we use actual scheduled seconds.

Luck score

For each spin, the casino's result varies by bet² × the game's payout variance. Adding that up over all spins gives the normal spread for a machine's total. Luck score = (house win − expected earnings) ÷ that spread. Across 310 machines the scores form the bell curve chance produces, except M-1158.

Luck simulator

Each game's payout per spin is modelled as 0 (no win), a regular win, or the top prize, with the three probabilities and sizes solved so the model matches the game's published RTP, payout variance and hit rate exactly. For each replay we draw the number of wins and top prizes for the machine's real number of spins, convert to dollars using its real amount bet, and correct for bet-size spread using Σbet². The share of 1,000 replays at or below the real result is a simulated p-value that does not rely on the bell-curve assumption behind the luck score.

Win-frequency check

The share of spins that pay anything, compared with the game's published hit probability. Over hundreds of thousands of spins, a 7-point gap isn't chance. That makes it a reason to inspect, not proof of a fault or of wrongdoing.

Machine verdicts

  • Inspect payout: luck score ≤ −3 and wins far more often than the game design.
  • Too new to judge: on the floor for less than half the selected days.
  • Watch: luck score ≤ −2.5 (about 2 of 310 machines by chance alone).
  • Quiet location: earns under 75% of the floor average per working hour, in Back Forty.
  • Low-earning game: the same, elsewhere (usually games designed to keep little, like Royal Draw).
  • Normal (luck): everything else.

Downtime pricing

For each machine-hour with downtime: lost earnings = seconds down × that machine's usual amount bet per working second at that hour × its expected keep rate.

Forecast, without peeking

Trained on Jan 1 – Apr 30, tested on May 1 – Jun 30. Event flags only include events announced by April 30, so Locals Appreciation Week (announced May 26) is not used. City tourism data is published later and is never an input. We also fit a linear regression (weekday dummies, event flag, linear trend; no lagged actuals, since a frozen 61-day forecast cannot see test-period results) and ran a rolling-origin backtest (forecast each of Mar, Apr, May, Jun from the months before it). The model was chosen on the Mar and Apr folds only.

Tracked vs. all play

Player figures cover loyalty-card play only. Concentration, the share of guests ahead, and comp ratios describe carded guests, not everyone.

Limits

  • The casino is fictional and its data is synthetic; nothing here describes a real property.
  • Supplied costs are partial (no rent, taxes or depreciation).
  • Action prices are given; their effects are our estimates, with uncertainty stated.
  • LVCVA and Missouri figures are real but published after 2025; context only.

Casino words, made simple

Amount bet
Everything guests wagered (coin-in).
Casino kept
Amount bet minus what machines paid back (house win).
Payback rate (RTP)
92% RTP means the casino expects to keep 8¢ per $1.
Expected earnings
What the casino should keep, with luck removed (theo).
Luck
Casino kept minus expected. Positive = casino was lucky.
Luck score
−2 to +2 is normal. Below −3 is too unlikely to be chance.
