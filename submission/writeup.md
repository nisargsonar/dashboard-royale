# Luck or Leak

**The question.** The Gilded Ace plans to replace its 10 lowest-earning slot machines. A machine can earn little because of bad luck, a quiet location, or a fault that pays out too much (a "leak"). We tested all three.

**What we found.**

- **Luck:** the floor kept within 0.4% of what game math predicts. Machine luck scores follow the bell curve chance predicts.
- **Location:** 9 of the 10 sit in Back Forty, where the same games earn 30–45% less per working hour. A walkway closure (Mar 3 to Apr 7) cut Back Forty foot traffic by 30%, and only 90% came back.
- **Leak:** M-1158 pays a win on 38.6% of spins against a 31.5% design, keeping 3.0% of bets instead of 10% ($36k short). In 1,000 simulated fair replays it never did this badly: a reason to inspect, not proof of wrongdoing.
- **Also:** two of the 10 were on the floor only about 50 days. Downtime cost just 0.4% of expected earnings. The top 10% of tracked guests carry 56% of losses. A May 1 comp change doubled the cost of unredeemed points without raising visits.

**Model.** A seasonal forecast of daily coin-in: weekday average × a boost for events announced in advance. Trained January to April, chosen on March–April back-tests, confirmed on May–June: average daily miss $47k vs. $218k for a flat-average baseline, and better in 4 of 4 test months. A regression did worse, so we kept the simple model. Decision: staff weekends and evenings, and test promotions on quiet days.

**Recommendation: spend $17,500, keep $82,500.**

1. **Inspect M-1158 and four next-lowest machines ($6,000).** Up to $5.8k recovered in July if a fault is fixed. Measure: hit rate back to 31.5%.
2. **Back Forty wayfinding ($7,500).** About $10.6k upside if the pre-closure gap closes; high uncertainty. Measure: Back Forty traffic and earnings per hour vs. other zones, before and after.
3. **Comp pilot with a control group ($4,000).** Tests whether the May 1 cut brings guests back; revert if not.

Replacing machines would not fix a quiet aisle.
